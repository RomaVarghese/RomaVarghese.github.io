01 / Ocean–ecosystem connections

Ocean variability.
Terrestrial response.

Tropical ocean teleconnections with vegetation productivity in India and monsoon Asia.

Doctoral research at IIT Kharagpur examined the influence of tropical sea-surface temperature variability on terrestrial vegetation, with emphasis on gross primary productivity (GPP) and satellite-observed solar-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF).

The research connects oceanic variability with hydroclimate anomalies and photosynthetic activity, including the Indian summer monsoon onset phase.

02 / Monsoon & basin hydrology

Tracing the pathways
of water.

Hydroclimatic controls on river-basin behaviour, vegetation and flood generation.

Research at IITM includes decadal prediction of Indian river-basin hydrology and the role of monsoon-driven hydrological pathways in semi-arid basins.

Related work examines the large-scale 2024 flood in the Krishna River basin and separates hydroclimatic and irrigation influences on vegetation photosynthesis variability in India.

03 / Earth observation & ecological modelling

Observations meet
predictive methods.

Bringing satellite measurements, field observations and artificial intelligence into ecological research.

An AI-based framework investigates vegetation photosynthesis anomalies in India associated with tropical oceanic variability. Additional studies use satellite observations to examine vegetation productivity and cyclone-related inundation.

Field experience includes mangrove biophysical measurements in Bhitarkanika and permanent vegetation plots around a pheno-meteorological observation site in the Similipal Biosphere Reserve, Odisha.

MTECH / OCEAN TECHNOLOGY

Chlorophyll in the southeastern Arabian Sea

Dissertation research used 18 years of satellite-derived chlorophyll-a and NASA Ocean Biogeochemical Model phytoplankton data to examine environmental change and phytoplankton community structure.

National Institute of Oceanography, Goa · 2017–2018

BTECH / CIVIL ENGINEERING

Groundwater quality through GIS mapping

Research in Varapuzha, Kerala, combined GIS mapping with laboratory analysis of drinking-water parameters in samples from 48 open wells, including assessment of iron and manganese.

Cochin University of Science and Technology
